Research

Computational design for sustainable permanent magnets.

I develop machine-learning and optimization methods that make materials prediction more useful, efficient, and reliable.

Research direction

My research focuses on computational materials design for permanent magnets. I study how machine learning, optimization, and simulation can help identify improved magnetic materials while reducing rare-earth content for more sustainable energy technologies.

At the Christian Doppler Laboratory for magnet design through physics-informed machine learning, I work on data-mining routines, predictive models, and their validation in collaboration with project partners.

How I approach the problem

Permanent-magnet design involves competing objectives: magnetic performance, cost, resource use, and manufacturability. I use computational models to explore these trade-offs and combine them with surrogate models when direct evaluations are expensive.

A central question is whether a model can be trusted where it is used. My work therefore evaluates uncertainty alongside prediction error and incorporates uncertainty-aware models into optimization workflows. This helps distinguish promising designs from predictions that require more information.

Trustworthy prediction

Uncertainty quantification

Predictive accuracy alone is not enough for scientific decision-making. I compare and develop diagnostics for model calibration, sharpness, and confidence so uncertainty can inform modeling and optimization choices.

Efficient design

Optimization & active learning

I investigate multi-objective optimization and active learning strategies for expensive objective functions, building modular and reproducible Python workflows for systematic comparison.

Research themes

  • Permanent-magnet materials and Nd–Fe–B systems
  • Physics-informed and uncertainty-aware machine learning
  • Surrogate models for materials-property prediction
  • Multi-objective optimization under competing material constraints
  • Active learning for expensive computational experiments
